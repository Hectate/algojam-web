# Craft algorithms that create artifacts.

**Creativity is endless!**

AlgoJam is the Algorithmic Generation Jam: a creative endeavor to show how computers can be used to make beautiful and interesting things, and then share them! Everything generative is welcome. It might be an entire game, a small part of one, or not a game at all. You might create something that produces music, poetry, art, or just a tool to make realistic and dynamic broken concrete.

## What kind of jam is this?

AlgoJam isn't a typical game jam. We've adjusted the structure so that more people can take part, and so you can spend your time on the interesting part: the generator.

- **You don't have to make a game.** A generator on its own is a perfectly good entry. Dungeons, planets, fabric patterns, music loops, alphabets, recipes, weird little poems: if your program makes something, it counts.
- **Bring what you already have.** You can use existing code, libraries, engines, art and music. You can even add a generative system to a project you've been working on for months. You don't need to start from scratch.
- **Make your own schedule.** Start a little early, finish a little late. Take breaks. There's no prize for crunching.
- **No ratings, no rankings, no winners.** A script that generates quilt patterns and a full murder mystery game can't be fairly compared, so we don't try. Instead, play each other's entries, leave comments and share what you love.

We want AlgoJam to be a melting pot of ideas, not a competition.

## Who is it for?

Everyone! AlgoJam is for:

- **First-timers** who have never written a generator (or never written code at all).
- **Game developers** who want to try a new technique, or add procedural generation to their project.
- **Artists, musicians and writers** who want to see what happens when a system does some of the making.
- **Tool builders** who want to make something other creators can use.
- **Researchers and students** who want a low-stakes place to prototype an idea. Feel free to bring your entire class!

## Ideas to get you started

Not sure what to make? Here are some things we've seen in the past to get you inspired:

- A **level, dungeon or world map** generator… and then make it weirder than "random levels."
- A **story or mystery generator** that simulates what happened, then lets the player piece it together.
- A **gallery** of generated paintings, buildings or creatures to wander through.
- A **writing partner** that takes turns with you on a poem.
- An **alphabet**, a **language**, a **shoe**, a **sword**, a **recipe**, a **constellation**.
- A **tool** that solves a problem other developers keep solving from scratch.
- A **happy little accident** you find beautiful.

## Push your generator further

Already comfortable with procedural generation? Here are some challenges to try:

- **Let it judge its own work.** Most generators are designed so they can never produce something bad. What if yours could make bad things, and could also *tell* when it had, then try again?
- **Let it explain itself.** Can your generator tell the player *why* it made what it made? A title, a description, a note from the "artist"?
- **Let it collaborate.** Build something that creates *with* a person, taking turns or making suggestions, rather than just for them.
- **Let players discover its range.** Make regenerating a part of the experience, so people learn the shape of your system by seeing lots of its output.
- **Let it be fragile.** Jams are the right place for ideas that are less reliable but more interesting.

## Share your work

AlgoJam is about sharing as much as making. When you submit, consider:

- **Writing a little about how it works.** A paragraph, a diagram, a devlog. Others will learn from it.
- **Sharing your source** if you're comfortable with that. A generator someone else can pick up and build on lasts long after the jam ends.
- **Playing and commenting on other entries.** Kind, specific feedback is the best prize this jam has.
- **Posting your progress** in the Discord as you go, including the broken outputs. Especially the broken outputs.

## AI usage

AlgoJam welcomes the use of generative AI tools as assistants, within some guidelines:

- AI tools may help you write or debug code, explain an algorithm, or bounce ideas around.
- Your submission should show **your** creativity. The idea, the design and the generator itself should come from you.
- Entries that are mostly AI output, such as software an agent designed and built from a prompt, or AI-generated images, music or text submitted as-is, aren't in the spirit of the jam and may be removed.
- **Say how you used AI tools.** A sentence or two on your submission page is enough.

If you'd rather work entirely by hand, that's welcome here too, and ProcJam, our older sibling, is a fully AI-free jam you should check out!

## A little history

ProcJam began in November 2014 with a day of talks and 138 entries: games, tools, toys and art. Its organizers chose not to run a typical game jam. Instead it asked people to make *something that makes something*, with no winners declared. AlgoJam carries that same spirit forward.

Read more about it in Michael Cook's report on the first ProcJam: [*Make Something That Makes Something: A Report On The First Procedural Generation Jam*](https://computationalcreativity.net/iccc2015/proceedings/9_1Cook.pdf) (ICCC 2015).
